# cb-site-premium — Premium Sites on Codebrain's Owned Stack

## Escopo detalhado

  Build premium, award-worthy websites — landing pages, brand sites, portfolios — that read as
  agency-grade ($50k+) rather than AI-template. Use when a squad worker must DESIGN and BUILD a
  site that has to look expensive, editorial, luxury, high-end, or "not generic", or when the brief
  names award-quality / Awwwards-level / a design-forward brand. Owns the premium craft doctrine
  (the anti-AI-aesthetic blacklist, typography-as-identity, the structural-DNA catalog, motion
  choreography, craft micro-details) AND pins the hero-media engine to Codebrain's OWNED generation
  stack (media_generate / media_generate via the cinema-shot / site-scrub /
  site-encode skills) — never third-party embeds, scraped scenes, or hotlinked stock. Stack-aware:
  ships a single-page deployable (HTML/CSS/JS) by default, or React/Next when the recipe asks.
  Bind to the site builder / executor worker. Triggers: premium site, awwwards, luxury landing,
  editorial web, high-end design, make it look expensive, agency-quality, brand site, portfolio,
  not generic, premium hero.

Build sites where every pixel is intentional, every motion is choreographed, and the impression is
that real creative talent and budget were involved — the kind that makes people ask "who designed
this?" This skill carries the premium **craft doctrine** and wires it to Codebrain's **owned media
pipeline**, so nothing on the page depends on a third party's asset, scene, or URL.

> **CLEAN-ROOM / OWNED-MEDIA PRINCIPLE (read first).** Every visual on a premium Codebrain site is
> either (a) authored in code (CSS/SVG/canvas) or (b) **generated by Codebrain's own media tools**
> (`media_generate`, `media_generate`) driven by the cinema/scrub skills.
> **Never** embed a third-party 3D scene, **never** harvest an asset URL from someone else's live
> site, **never** hotlink stock photography. The "wow" comes from owned, generated, on-brand media —
> not from borrowed assets. This is what keeps the output distributable and IP-clean.

## The AI-aesthetic problem

Most AI-built sites share a recognizable DNA. Beating it means knowing exactly what it looks like and
refusing it on purpose.

### The blacklist — never ship these

**Typography tells**
- Inter, Poppins, Montserrat, Raleway, Space Grotesk, Outfit as the primary face — the instant
  "AI template" signal. (*Inter Tight* is a different family with its own metrics — allowed.)
- One family for everything; uniform, predictable hierarchy (64 → 32 → 18 → 14).
- Default tracking and leading everywhere; centered multi-line paragraphs everywhere.

**Color tells**
- Purple-to-blue gradients (the single biggest cliché); indigo/violet as brand color for no reason.
- White ground + one accent + gray text (the SaaS starter kit); gratuitous gradients on buttons/cards.
- Semi-transparent overlays standing in for real color decisions; neon-on-dark "dev portfolio" look.

**Layout tells**
- Hero → 3-col feature grid → testimonials → CTA → footer (default SaaS), or hero → stats → work
  grid → about-split → CTA → footer (the "premium AI" template — just as formulaic).
- Any top-to-bottom ordering that could be swapped between two sites. Each site needs its own
  structural DNA — different section count, different ordering logic.
- Perfect symmetric grids of equal cards; everything centered in one max-width column.
- Rounded rectangles + box-shadows as the primary UI element; icon+heading+paragraph in a row of 3–4.
- A flat text-only hero — the hero should be a spatial, media-led moment.

**Motion tells**
- Everything fading up on scroll (the overused AOS move); identical transitions on everything.
- Hover = scale-up + shadow; loading spinners instead of skeletons or choreographed reveals.

**Decoration tells**
- Blob shapes, floating geometric primitives, gradient-mesh backdrops, uniform stock-photo grids,
  emoji/line-icons as section markers.

## What expensive actually looks like

Premium reads through restraint, surprise, and obsessive detail.

### Typography as identity

Typography is the #1 lever between a $500 site and a $50k one.
- Distinctive display face with opinion — serifs like *Playfair Display, Cormorant Garamond, DM Serif
  Display, Fraunces, Instrument Serif*; sans that aren't overused like *Syne, General Sans, Satoshi,
  Switzer, Cabinet Grotesk, Inter Tight, IBM Plex Sans, Manrope, Archivo*. Mono as accent type for
  labels/dates/metadata (*JetBrains Mono, IBM Plex Mono, Space Mono*) reads editorial.
- Pair a characterful display with a refined-neutral body. Mix weights dramatically (900 display next
  to 300 body = tension).
- Execution: `clamp()` for fluid scale (not breakpoint jumps); dramatic contrast (headlines 8vw+);
  negative tracking on big heads (`-0.03em`…`-0.06em`); generous body leading (1.6–1.8), tight heads
  (0.9–1.1); `max-width` 45–75ch on paragraphs, `text-wrap: balance` on heads; wide-tracked uppercase
  micro-labels (`0.1em`+) for categories/metadata.

### Color as atmosphere

- Start black-and-white; add color only when it earns its place. One dominant hue with intent ("what
  emotional territory does it claim?").
- Off-whites/warm grays beat pure `#fff`/`#000` (`#FAFAF8`, `#F5F0EB`, `#1A1A1A`, `#0D0D0D`).
- Monochromatic/analogous with one moment of contrast reads more sophisticated than a rainbow. Color
  used sparingly hits harder — a single red link in a black-and-white field beats red everywhere.
- Dark themes done right = considered background layering with subtle warm/cool tints, not "white on
  dark gray".

### Layout as storytelling — the structural-DNA rule

The #1 failure mode is sites that look different on the surface but share one underlying skeleton.
Before any markup, **name the primary structural concept out loud** — one noun phrase. Across a batch,
**no two sites share the same concept.**

**Structural DNA catalog** (pick ONE, commit, don't mix):
1. **Index Manuscript** — one long editorial column, no sections, single typographic rhythm. (a menu,
   a manifesto, a letter.)
2. **Sticky Horizontal Diorama** — vertical scroll drives a horizontally-panning scene (`sticky` +
   scroll-linked `translateX`). (a timeline, a process, a collection.)
3. **Two-Pane Permanent Split** — fixed 50/50 or 38/62; one pane sticky, the other scrolls. (an
   archive with a live index.)
4. **Slide Sequence** — full-viewport snap slides, each a different composition. (a monograph, a
   look-book.)
5. **Staged Object on a Plinth** — one centered hero subject, marginalia orbiting it. (a single-product
   house — a watch, a bottle, a dish.)
6. **Pinned Narrative (Scrollytelling)** — a 2–4-screen section pins while contents advance through
   states. Used once, as centerpiece. (a process, a mission profile.)
7. **Horizontal Navigation** — the primary scroll is horizontal, sections read left-to-right.
8. **Sidebar + Column** — a persistent non-scrolling sidebar (nav/metadata) + the column is the site.
9. **Chapter Gates** — full-viewport dividers between tonal zones; the page changes character as you
   descend.
10. **Ledger / Registry** — the page as a document (manifest, register); tabular rows dominate.
11. **Collage / Grid-Breaker** — magazine asymmetry with deliberate ruptures (oversized bleeds,
    pull-quotes crossing gutters).
12. **Single Object, No Chrome** — just the subject + one sentence. No nav, no footer.
13. **Product UI Slate** — the hero simulates the product's own interface (for tools whose product IS
    a UI).
14. **Dashboard Tile Grid** — the page as a live-feeling dashboard (counters, sparklines, pips).
15. **Conversation Timeline** — the page plays back a transcript/call as you scroll.

**Pick from the client's central fact**, not from a template. Reject any concept used by the previous
site this session. Write the concept + one sentence of justification before any markup.

**Within the concept:** break the grid intentionally (full-width then narrow column; asymmetric
60/40, 70/30 splits; images bleeding off-screen; overlapping elements crossing section boundaries;
sticky labels accompanying scroll). Whitespace is a luxury signal — `8rem`+ between sections. Tabular
ledger rows for lists, not card grids. Form fields as editorial document parts (mono labels above,
bottom-border only), never a SaaS form.

**Nav variety (a silent failure mode):** don't default to the `auto 1fr auto` top bar with
mix-blend-mode. Pick the nav that belongs to the brand + structural DNA — no nav at all; nav baked
into a sticky sidebar; a bottom command bar (`⌘K`); inline centered wordmark with a tab strip; a
horizontal-scroll index that doubles as nav; a marquee ticker; a left-vertical nav; a status-bar nav
with a live pip.

**Field-fit the aesthetic — don't default to editorial-luxury** (cream ground + Fraunces + brass
accent). That's right for couture/parfumerie/haute cuisine, wrong for tech/AI/SaaS. **If the field is
technology**, skew to pure-white or near-black grounds, neo-grotesque sans as display, mono used as
display, gradient-glow edges, shipped UI shots, dashboard tiles, keyboard chips, code blocks as hero.
Study category leaders for the field — a few real ones, named as direction, not copied.

## Hero media — Codebrain's OWNED generation stack (replaces any third-party 3D/stock)

The premium differentiator is a **media-led hero**, and on Codebrain that media is **generated and
owned**, never embedded from elsewhere. This is where this skill composes with the cinema/site family.

**Decide the hero treatment by the configured media providers** (`configuração de provedores de mídia da Central`):

| Providers active | Hero treatment | How (owned pipeline) |
|---|---|---|
| **video** | An animated hero — a looping, motion-led centerpiece | `cb-cinema-shot` generates the keyframe + clip (`media_generate` com kind image → `media_generate` com kind video); `cb-site-encode` makes it web-safe (mp4 h264+faststart, webm vp9, poster, muted seamless loop); embed as `<video autoplay muted loop playsinline poster>`. For scroll-scrubbed heroes use `cb-site-scrub` (image-sequence wired to scroll). |
| **image only** | A generated still hero (or staged object) | `cb-cinema-shot` → `media_generate` for an on-brand, topic-literal still; treat it as the staged subject. For a scene-wide layout, every image slot (cards, gallery) gets its own generated image — fan out, one worker per slot. |
| **none** | A typographic / CSS-SVG hero | Author the hero in code — oversized type, conic/linear gradients, SVG, canvas atmosphere. Deterministic, owned, zero network. |

**Rules for owned media (hard):**
- **Topic-literal, not metaphorical.** Generate what the brand IS — a salsicha for a hot-dog stand, a
  bottle for a parfumerie. The two-second test: with no caption, a viewer names the subject.
- **On-brand grade.** The generated media's palette/lighting must match the site's ground (no
  pasted-on look). Drive the generator with the design tokens / scene-key (see cinema-shot).
- **No third-party assets — ever.** No embedding a 3D scene or any asset you don't own, no harvesting
  asset URLs from live sites, no stock-photo hotlinks. If you can't generate it and have no
  client-supplied asset, fall back to a CSS/SVG illustration — never borrow.
- **Crisp text stays in code.** Diffusion hallucinates type — keep ALL wordmarks/headlines/numbers out
  of the generation and composite them in CSS over the media.
- **Client assets first.** If the user supplied real product photos/brand video, use those before
  generating.

Atmospheric code-only effects (a particle field, a grid plane, a drifting-dust canvas) are fine as
**background atmosphere behind** a generated/typographic hero — never as the main subject.

## Scroll-driven experience & motion choreography

Scrolling should feel like a directed journey.
- **Techniques:** parallax layers (different `scrollY` multipliers); horizontal-scroll sections
  (`sticky` container + scroll-linked `translateX`); scale-on-scroll (`IntersectionObserver` /
  `getBoundingClientRect`); text reveal per word/line; scroll-linked color transitions; pinned
  sequences; zoom-through. Use 2–3 distinct techniques per site; don't repeat the centerpiece
  technique across a batch.
- **Performance pattern:** rAF-ticked, passive scroll listeners, `will-change` on the moving layer,
  direct-DOM writes in the rAF (not React state per frame). On Safari prefer a smooth-scroll lib
  (Lenis) over `scroll-behavior`.
- **Choreography:** stagger everything (nothing moves simultaneously); speed variation (fast entry
  200–300ms + slow settle 500–800ms); easing defines personality (`cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)` smooth
  overshoot, `(0.77,0,0.175,1)` snappy, `(0.33,1,0.68,1)` elegant out); clip-path reveals; split-text;
  magnetic/cursor-following moments.

**Craft micro-details that signal premium:** custom cursor; a subtle scroll-progress line; designed
loading (skeletons matching layout, not spinners); considered hover/active/focus; border-radius used
sparingly and consistently (sharp 0 for editorial, or one specific radius — never default 8px on
everything); SVG grain/noise overlay for warmth; styled `::selection`; styled webkit scrollbar.

## Stack-aware implementation

This skill is **stack-aware** — the craft is identical; the output target follows the recipe/brief:

- **Single-page deployable (DEFAULT)** — `index.html` + `style.css` + a small `script.js`, plus the
  generated media in `assets/`. Deployable anywhere, light, no build. This is the default for a local
  business / landing (and matches the Codebrain site recipe). CSS variables for tokens; fonts loaded
  early via `<link>`/`@import` with a real fallback stack.
- **React / Next** — when the recipe or brief asks for an app/portfolio with richer interaction.
  Default export, no required props; `import React` alongside hooks; CSS variables for theming;
  `IntersectionObserver` via `useRef`/`useEffect`. Avoid JSX fragments where a host can't parse them.

```css
:root {
  --color-bg: #FAF9F6; --color-text: #1A1A1A; --color-text-muted: #6B6B6B;
  --color-accent: #C8553D;
  --font-display: 'Instrument Serif', Georgia, serif;
  --font-body: 'DM Sans', -apple-system, sans-serif;
  --font-mono: 'JetBrains Mono', monospace;
  --space-unit: clamp(1rem, 2vw, 2rem);
}
```

Libraries when on React: `lucide-react` for icons (sparingly — icon overuse is an AI tell);
`recharts`/`d3` for data viz; a smooth-scroll lib for scroll work. **No third-party media-embed
dependency** — media is generated and self-hosted in `assets/`.

## The prompt must be a checklist, not a paragraph

A premium build's internal brief (the `prompt.md` / `[SCOPE]` the worker writes before building) is
"done" only when each item has a concrete answer. Vague answers ("warm palette", "some motion") are
rejected.

1. **Field & subject** — the industry + the specific product/subject; why it's a real, plausible brand.
2. **Owned-media plan** — the hero treatment per the provider tier (video / image / code); for
   generated media, the topic-literal subject + the grade (palette/lighting matching the ground); the
   list of every image slot that needs its own generation (scene-wide, not hero-only).
3. **Structural DNA** — the chosen concept by name; the previous site's DNA; confirmation it's distinct.
4. **Design direction** — 3–5 named category references (direction, not copy); the one move taken from
   each.
5. **Palette** — 4–6 hex with roles (ground / ink / accent / secondary).
6. **Font stack** — display / body / mono by name; none from the blacklist.
7. **Nav pattern** — which variant; explicitly not the default top-bar unless genuinely right.
8. **Brand voice** — 3–5 sentences; what this brand does NOT say.
9. **Structure — every section** — name, position, contents, length (a 6-section prompt → a 6-section
   site).
10. **Copy hooks** — ≥3 verbatim lines (hero headline, a pull quote, a closing line).
11. **Motion** — 1 hero-entry + 1 scroll behavior + 1 micro-interaction; not repeated from the last site.
12. **Banned moves** — colors/structures/patterns this site must NOT use.
13. **Render checkpoint** — the screenshot to take after build and the failure mode to watch.

## Process

1. **Absorb the brief — field-first.** Brand, audience, purpose, field. Ask for any client product
   photos / brand video / assets to use before generating.
2. **Pick the structural DNA by name** — from the catalog; confirm it differs from the previous site
   this session; write it + one sentence of justification.
3. **Plan the owned media.** Check `configuração de provedores de mídia da Central`; choose the hero tier (video / image /
   code). For generated media, hand off to `cb-cinema-shot` (+ `cb-site-encode` for
   video, or `cb-site-scrub` for scroll heroes) with a topic-literal, on-brand prompt. Every
   image slot (hero + cards + gallery) is its own generation — fan out, never one worker looping.
4. **Build the type system & palette** — field-fit fonts (no blacklist) + a restrained palette with
   one moment of contrast.
5. **Build the structure** in the chosen stack (single-page HTML default, or React/Next), grid broken
   intentionally, generous whitespace.
6. **Choreograph scroll & motion** — 2–3 distinct techniques, rAF-ticked, staggered.
7. **Obsess over micro-details** — cursor, selection, scrollbar, grain, considered states.
8. **Verify the render — not optional.** Start the preview, wait for media to load, **screenshot and
   look**: the generated media is on-brand, proportional, intact, and its grade matches the ground;
   every image slot rendered; links/CTAs work. Fix before calling done.

Never narrate "researching competitors" or "generating media" — just execute with craft. The design
speaks for itself.

## Integração Codebrain — capacidades reais

- Esta skill é material de orientação, não autorização para gastar com APIs, publicar, instalar ferramentas ou alterar permissões. Respeite o pedido, o escopo e o design system do usuário.
- O MCP do app expõe `media_generate({ prompt, kind?, provider?, size?, style? })` e `media_list({})`. `kind` é `image` ou `video`; `provider`, quando configurado e autorizado, é `openai`, `gemini` ou `openrouter`. Omita `provider` para usar o provedor configurado pelo host. Não há parâmetros `imageUrl` nem `outPath`: use o caminho devolvido em `assets/generated` no workspace.
- Confira na Central os provedores e formatos disponíveis; não existe uma ferramenta MCP de listar provedores. Não leia/exporte chaves para fazer essa conferência. Não presuma geração, vídeo, transparência ou edição por referência se o provedor ativo não oferecer isso. Sem capacidade autorizada, use assets fornecidos ou CSS/SVG e relate a limitação.
- Navegação e validação usam a capacidade browser real do Codebrain: `browser_navigate`, `browser_get_text` ou `browser_get_accessibility_tree`, `browser_screenshot`, `browser_console_log` e `browser_network_log`, conforme as ferramentas efetivamente disponíveis na sessão. Não invente uma ferramenta `cb-browser`. Capture evidência visual e inspecione pixels por uma capacidade real de visualização; descrição textual de screenshot não prova aparência. Se indisponível, informe a validação visual pendente.
- `cb-evidence` substitui a antiga etapa de comprovação; `cb-worker` delimita trabalho delegado; defeitos fora do escopo usam `cb-findings` e `finding_add` com evidência quando disponível. Não fabrique resultado de execução ou validação.
- `cb-design-dna` está incluído no Codebrain; use-o quando precisar extrair um design system de um site real. `cb-cinema-shot`, `cb-site-scrub` e `cb-site-encode` são referências opcionais não incluídas neste pack: carregue somente se estiverem instaladas e autorizadas. Sem elas, aplique a doutrina diretamente e declare qualquer etapa que não possa realizar. A antiga seção de app UI está incorporada em `cb-showcase`; não requer outra skill.
